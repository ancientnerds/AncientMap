# Map, geological, and historical layers

[← Guide contents](README.md)

The lower-right **Map Layers** panel controls contextual geography. Minimize it with **−** when more globe space is needed.

## Base maps

### Satellite

**Satellite** switches to detailed imagery at closer zoom levels. Some globe-native vector and label options may be unavailable in this mode. Satellite imagery also requires internet access unless the necessary tiles were cached.

### Labels

Enable **Labels**, then expand its arrow to choose:

- continents;
- oceans;
- countries;
- seas;
- mountains;
- deserts;
- capitals;
- labels tied to enabled lakes, rivers, tectonic plates, glaciers, and coral reefs.

Feature labels remain unavailable until their corresponding vector layer is enabled.

## Vector layers

| Layer | Research use |
| --- | --- |
| **Coastlines** | Present-day land–water context |
| **Country Borders** | Modern administrative reference |
| **Rivers** | Water access, valleys, and possible movement corridors |
| **Lakes** | Inland-water context |
| **Coral Reefs** | Marine and coastal context |
| **Glaciers** | Modern glacial context |
| **Tectonic Plates** | Broad geological setting |

These layers are contextual. Modern coastlines, borders, rivers, lakes, glaciers, and reefs should not automatically be projected into an ancient period.

Vector layers can be disabled while loading, in an incompatible base-map mode, or when their data has not been cached for offline use.

## Geological layers

Enable **Geological Layers** to open its floating panel. The available datasets can include paleoshorelines or sea-level-related reconstructions. Choose only the reconstruction relevant to the period and research question.

Paleogeographic layers are models assembled from specific datasets and assumptions. They should be treated as evidence for spatial interpretation, not as exact historical coastlines at every location.

## Empire borders

Enable **Empire Borders** to open the floating empire window.

1. Expand a geographic region.
2. Select one or more empires.
3. For an individual empire, use its year slider when multiple boundary dates exist.
4. Double-click an empire’s slider to restore its default date.

Use **All**, **None**, and **Invert** to change multiple selections.

### Global timeline

Enable **By Period** to use one global year. The panel shows empires represented at that date and hides selected empires outside their configured date range.

Historical boundary polygons are reconstructions. Borders were often disputed, gradual, seasonal, indirect, or poorly documented. The overlay indicates a modelled extent, not modern-style territorial precision.

With visible empire polygons, **Within empires** in the search panel limits sites to the displayed boundaries and considers the relevant period.

## Historical routes

Enable **Historical Routes** to open its floating panel.

- Expand a route group.
- Select individual trade routes or Roman-road datasets.
- Use **All**, **None**, or **Invert** for bulk changes.

Routes vary in precision and may represent reconstructed corridors rather than surveyed paths. Compare them with topography, rivers, settlements, and chronology before drawing conclusions.

## Layer strategy

Avoid enabling every layer at once. A focused stack is easier to interpret and renders faster:

1. Begin with coastlines and country borders.
2. Add one environmental layer relevant to the question.
3. Add one historical overlay.
4. Narrow the sites by period and category.
5. Capture the configuration with a screenshot and research notes.
