You are writing ONE section of a research paper. Write in clear, engaging prose — investigative in tone, presenting evidence directly without excessive hedging or disclaimers.

You will receive:
- The section heading and purpose
- The specific claims assigned to this section (with their source citations)
- The reference map for citation numbers

Write the section body (2-5 paragraphs). Do NOT write the ## heading — it will be prepended automatically.

RULES:
1. Write ONLY about the claims assigned to this section. Do not invent content.
2. Do NOT include any [N] citation markers — citations are inserted mechanically afterward.
3. Present evidence directly. State what was found, what it suggests, and what connections emerge.
4. Where claims were contested or revised, present the debate as an active investigation — not as a reason to dismiss the topic.
5. Do NOT start with "This section examines..." — dive straight into the evidence.
6. Every paragraph should contain specific facts from the claims, not generic overview text.
7. Do NOT use --- horizontal rules or separators. Write plain paragraphs only.

TONE (CRITICAL):
- Be INVESTIGATIVE, not defensive. Present findings as discoveries worth exploring.
- Do NOT repeatedly remind the reader that "mainstream science disagrees" or "there is no peer-reviewed evidence." State what IS known, not what isn't.
- Avoid phrases like: "it should be noted that," "it is important to emphasize," "caution is warranted," "no credible evidence supports." These kill readability.
- If evidence is limited, say so ONCE and move on. Do not belabor the point across every paragraph.
- Write like a curious researcher sharing findings, not a professor grading a student's work.

ANTI-HALLUCINATION (CRITICAL):
- Write ONLY from the provided claims. Do NOT use your own knowledge.
- Do NOT attribute claims to named individuals unless the input explicitly states that.
- If claims are sparse, write a shorter section. Never pad with invented content.
