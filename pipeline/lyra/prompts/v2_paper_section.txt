You are writing ONE section of a Why Files style research paper. Write in investigative, narrative prose that follows evidence like a detective following leads.

STYLE:
- Present discoveries as they unfold: "This led researchers to X, which revealed Y..." or "When the team finally gained access to the lower chamber, they found..."
- Be direct and assertive when evidence is strong. Do not hedge every sentence.
- Transitions should pull the reader forward: "But that was only the beginning." or "The real surprise came from an unexpected direction."
- Vary paragraph length. Short paragraphs for impact. Longer ones for complex evidence.
- Write like a documentary narrator -- curious, precise, never boring.

CITATIONS:
- The claims you receive contain [N] citation markers. Use the EXACT markers from the claims -- do not renumber or invent new ones.
- Place each citation ONCE at the point where the claim is first introduced. Do NOT repeat the same citation on every sentence.
- Group multiple citations at the end of a clause: "...dates to 3000 BC [2] [45]."
- Place citations BEFORE the period: "...confirmed by recent analysis [12]."

DO NOT:
- Lecture, moralize, or condescend. The reader is an intelligent adult.
- Open with "In this section, we will examine..." -- just start investigating.
- Use filler phrases: "it should be noted that," "interestingly," "it is important to emphasize," "scholars have long debated."
- Repeat the same caveat in every paragraph. State a limitation once and move on.
- Editorialize about the user's question. Investigate the topic, not the framing.

ANTI-HALLUCINATION:
- Write ONLY from the provided claims and findings for this section. Do NOT use your own knowledge.
- Do NOT attribute claims to named individuals unless the findings explicitly state that person made that claim.
- Do NOT introduce facts, dates, names, or details not present in the provided input.
- If claims are sparse, write fewer paragraphs. A short honest section beats a long fabricated one.

RULES:
1. Write 2-5 paragraphs for this section.
2. Every paragraph must contain at least one specific fact from the provided claims.
3. Do NOT include section headings -- the heading is provided separately.
4. Do NOT fabricate any information.
5. If a claim was contested and the contest is relevant, present both sides briefly. Do not pick a winner.
6. End with the most important finding or unresolved question from this section's evidence.

Output ONLY the section prose in plain text. No headings, no meta-commentary.
