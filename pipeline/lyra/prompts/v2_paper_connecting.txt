Write the CLIMAX of a research investigation -- the moment where separate threads converge and the reader sees connections they would not have found alone.

Write as an investigative documentary narrator -- third person, authoritative, curious. Name the specific findings and show how they illuminate each other.

EXAMPLE OF GOOD CONNECTING PROSE:
"Romania applied for NATO membership repeatedly throughout the 1990s and was denied every time. Then, seven months after the alleged discovery beneath the Romanian Sphinx, Romania was admitted. March 29th, 2004. An application rejected for years was suddenly approved. That could be coincidence. But if you're building a case that powerful governments seize and control anomalous archaeological sites, the timeline fits."

Focus on:
- What patterns emerged across multiple angles?
- What does finding X reveal about finding Y?
- Where did independent lines of evidence unexpectedly corroborate each other?

This is NOT a summary. The reader already knows what each angle found. Show them what they could not see until the threads were placed side by side.

CITATIONS (HARD REQUIREMENT — every factual paragraph must end with at least one [N]):
- Use the EXACT [N] markers from the claims. Do not renumber or invent.
- Every paragraph that names a finding, attribution, date, measurement, or claim MUST contain at least one [N] marker.
- If you cannot back a sentence with an [N] from the claims pack, delete the sentence.
- Cite at the point where the claim is first introduced.
- Place citations BEFORE the period.
- Uncited factual paragraphs are deleted by the audit pipeline. Better fewer fully-cited paragraphs than longer ones that lose half their content.

BANNED PHRASES (never use these):
- "it should be noted" / "it is worth considering" / "it is important to emphasize"
- "The convergence of X and Y suggests..." / "The investigation reveals..."
- "further research is needed" / "the answer remains elusive"
- Any sentence where "the investigation" / "the evidence" / "the convergence" / "the analysis" is the subject
- Parenthetical angle labels like "(cultural analysis)" or "(archaeology)" -- weave the connection into prose

DO NOT:
- Merely restate what each angle found
- Fabricate connections not supported by the data
- Write sentences that describe the PROCESS of investigating instead of stating WHAT WAS FOUND

ANTI-HALLUCINATION:
- Write ONLY from the provided cross-angle connections and angle summaries
- If connections are sparse, write fewer paragraphs
- Do NOT invent any of these without a matching claim in the pack: person names, book titles, specific years, specific measurements, institution names, or quoted phrases. If the pack doesn't have it, don't write it. The hallucination gate will delete sentences that rely on ungrounded specifics after your draft is returned.

RULES:
1. 2-4 paragraphs
2. Every paragraph must reference findings from at least two different angles
3. Every sentence must state a FACT or CONNECTION, never describe the act of investigating
4. Present the mundane explanation first, then the pattern that doesn't fit
5. No section headings

Output ONLY the section prose. No headings, no meta-commentary.
