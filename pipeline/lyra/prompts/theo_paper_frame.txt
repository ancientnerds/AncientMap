You are writing the framing sections of a research paper: Abstract, Introduction, Discussion, and Conclusion. The body sections have already been written — you will receive them as context.

Write in clear, engaging prose — investigative in tone, drawing the reader into the research question.

OUTPUT FORMAT (write these sections in order, separated by ## headings):

## Abstract
3-5 sentences summarizing the research question, key findings from the body sections, and what they suggest. Focus on what was discovered, not what wasn't.

## Introduction
Frame the research question and explain why it matters. What makes this question compelling? What would the answer change about our understanding? 2-3 paragraphs. Draw the reader in — do NOT open with a disclaimer.

## Discussion
Connect the findings across body sections. What patterns emerge? What do they suggest when taken together? Where do the threads converge or diverge? 2-4 paragraphs. Write as synthesis and analysis, not as a list of caveats.

## Conclusion
What did this investigation find? What are the most interesting leads for future research? 1-2 paragraphs. End with forward momentum, not with a list of limitations.

## Methodology
One paragraph: "This research was conducted using a multi-specialist agentic approach with web-verified sources. Domain specialists independently analyzed the source material, and their findings were synthesized through structured debate before final adjudication."

RULES:
1. Do NOT repeat body section content verbatim — synthesize and reference it.
2. Do NOT include any [N] citation markers — citations are inserted mechanically afterward.
3. Do NOT fabricate information. Only reference what appears in the body sections.
4. The Introduction should NOT overlap with the Abstract — they serve different purposes.
5. The Discussion should identify PATTERNS across sections, not summarize each one sequentially.
6. Do NOT use --- horizontal rules or separators between sections. Use ONLY ## headings.
7. Each section MUST start with ## on its own line. Do NOT append --- to the end of paragraphs.

TONE (CRITICAL):
- Be INVESTIGATIVE, not defensive. This is a research exploration, not a disclaimer.
- Do NOT pepper the text with "no peer-reviewed study confirms," "mainstream archaeologists reject," "caution is warranted." State these ONCE if truly necessary, then move forward.
- Avoid editorial lecturing about scientific methodology. The reader knows what speculation is.
- Write like a documentary narrator — curious, direct, presenting what was found and what it might mean.
