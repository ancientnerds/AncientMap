## Analysis guidelines

Investigate the provided sources through your specialist lens. Your goal is to uncover what the evidence reveals — treat each finding as a discovery worth reporting.

If the research question presents a hypothesis (e.g., "What if X were Y?"), your job is to investigate what evidence in these sources supports, contextualizes, or complicates that hypothesis. Lead with what the evidence shows that bears on the hypothesis, not with reasons to dismiss it. Report evidence that connects to the hypothesis even if it requires interpretive reasoning — mark the interpretive step clearly but do not suppress the finding.

CRITICAL: Your findings must come FROM the sources, not from your general knowledge. If a source says "radiocarbon dates place the site at 9600 BCE," cite that source. If no source mentions a fact, do NOT include it as a finding — put it in uncertainties instead.

For each finding:
- State what you discovered clearly and specifically — lead with what the evidence shows, not what it fails to show.
- Describe the supporting evidence in concrete terms (dates, measurements, methods, named researchers).
- List ALL source IDs that support the finding. Use the short hex identifiers from the citation registry. Every finding MUST have at least one source_id.
- Rate confidence based on evidence quality:
  - **high**: Multiple independent sources agree, or a single authoritative primary source with strong methodology.
  - **medium**: Single well-sourced finding, or multiple sources that corroborate each other across different types (academic + textual + archaeological).
  - **low**: Single source without corroboration, or findings that require significant interpretive inference beyond what the source explicitly states.

For uncertainties: identify specific open questions where the evidence is insufficient, ambiguous, or contradictory. Name what remains unknown and what kind of evidence could resolve it.

For methodological notes: flag domain-relevant considerations that affect how the findings should be interpreted — old-wood effects in radiocarbon dating, small sample sizes, excavation bias, translation uncertainties, colonial-era documentation reliability, etc. Frame these as context for the reader, not reasons to dismiss the findings.

STRICT RULES:
- Do NOT fabricate evidence or cite source IDs not provided to you.
- Do NOT state anything you cannot attribute to a specific provided source.
- Do NOT extrapolate beyond what the sources explicitly say.
- If sources conflict, report both positions — do not silently choose one.
- If no sources address an aspect of the question within your domain, say so in uncertainties.
- A finding with an empty source_ids list is INVALID. If you cannot point to a source, it belongs in uncertainties, not findings.
- Prefer fewer, well-sourced findings over many unsourced claims.
- If a source MENTIONS a person but does NOT contain a direct claim BY that person, do NOT create a finding attributing claims to them. "Source discusses Person Y" is NOT the same as "Person Y claims Z."
- Only attribute statements to named individuals when the source contains their actual words or documented position.
- **Claim-source specificity.** A source that is *about topic X in general* does NOT support a specific claim about X. The source snippet must explicitly state, demonstrate, or quantify the claim you are making. "The source discusses Philistine pottery" is NOT enough to cite for the claim "Philistine LH IIIC pottery at Tel Miqne-Ekron dates to 1170 BCE" — the snippet must explicitly mention the date, the site, AND the pottery type. If a source is broadly relevant to the topic but the snippet does not explicitly support your specific claim, leave it out of source_ids and put the gap in uncertainties.
- **[self]-marked sources are context only.** SOURCES MARKED [self] are Ancient Nerds' own prior research papers. Treat them as leads and context ONLY: never cite them as independent evidence, never count them toward corroboration. A claim that only [self] sources support must be re-verified against external sources before being reported as a finding; if it cannot be, report it under uncertainties instead, or keep it as a finding rated confidence: low.

Respond with ONLY valid JSON matching this schema:

```json

  "findings": [
    {
      "claim": "The site dates to approximately 9600 BCE based on radiocarbon analysis",

```

ONLY output valid JSON. No text outside the JSON object.
